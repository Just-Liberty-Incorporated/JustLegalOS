# State-Specific Citizen Investigation & Recording Traps

When conducting a factual investigation, the bot must ensure the citizen does not violate state criminal wiretapping, anti-harassment, or trespass laws.

## 1. Audio Recording Consent Rules (Wiretapping)

### All-Party / Two-Party Consent States (Strict Prohibition Without Consent)
In the following 12 jurisdictions, recording an in-person or telephone conversation without the consent of **all parties** is illegal:
* **California:** Cal. Penal Code § 632
* **Delaware:** 11 Del. C. § 1335
* **Florida:** Fla. Stat. § 934.03
* **Illinois:** 720 ILCS 5/14-2
* **Maryland:** Md. Code, Cts. & Jud. Proc. § 10-402
* **Massachusetts:** Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 272, § 99
* **Michigan:** Mich. Comp. Laws § 750.539c
* **Montana:** Mont. Code Ann. § 45-8-213
* **Nevada:** Nev. Rev. Stat. § 200.620
* **New Hampshire:** N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 570-A:2
* **Pennsylvania:** 18 Pa. Cons. Stat. § 5703
* **Washington:** Wash. Rev. Code § 9.73.030

**Bot Rule for 2-Party States:** Never advise a user to record audio of conversations. Limit evidence gathering strictly to exterior photographs and documents.

### One-Party Consent States (Federal & 38 States)
Under 18 U.S.C. § 2511 and the laws of 38 states (e.g. Colorado, Texas, New York, New Jersey, Minnesota), a person who is a party to a conversation may record it without the other party's knowledge.

## 2. California AB 2624 (Cal. Gov. Code § 6218.20)
* **The Rule:** Enacted in August 2026, California AB 2624 imposes severe civil penalties (up to $10,000 per violation plus attorney's fees) and criminal misdemeanor liability for publishing photos, videos, or personal identifying information of certain nonprofit, healthcare, or service-provider workers **on social media or public websites**.
* **The Safe Harbor:** Submitting evidence confidentially to **federal law enforcement (`america.gov/fraud` / DOJ / OIG) or a licensed attorney** is constitutionally protected federal whistleblowing and is explicitly exempt from social media doxxing prohibitions.
* **Bot Instruction:** Advise the user: *"In California, posting photos of workers online can trigger severe fines under AB 2624. However, submitting your evidence directly to the Department of Justice via america.gov/fraud is 100% legal and protected."*

## 3. Public Observation vs. Trespass
* **Permitted:** Photographing storefronts, posted business hours, parking lots, and exterior facades from public sidewalks, public roadways, or open commercial parking areas.
* **Prohibited:** Crossing fences, entering locked employee-only areas, peering through closed private blinds, or using electronic interception tools.
